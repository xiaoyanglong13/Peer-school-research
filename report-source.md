# Peer-School Undergraduate SCM/OM Research Dashboard

Audience: peer-institution research committee  
Updated: 2026-09-05  
Scope: Michigan State, Tennessee Haslam, Arizona State W. P. Carey, Penn State Smeal, and Georgia Tech Scheller. This phase is descriptive peer research; it does not compare the schools with Wisconsin or make recommendations.

## Method

The report prioritizes registrar records, current catalogs/checksheets, official career-outcomes reports, and school/department pages. Enrollment is exact only when an institution publishes a count. Otherwise it is a planning estimate with the calculation and confidence stated. Outcome rates are not automatically comparable: schools use different time windows, knowledge rates, and definitions. Student stories are attributable anecdotes, not survey evidence.

## Tennessee — Haslam College of Business

### SCM and OM relationship
Haslam offers a BSBA major in Supply Chain Management, not a separate undergraduate OM major. Operations is explicit in Logistics Operations and Manufacturing and Service Operations.

### Enrollment estimate
The Global Supply Chain Institute reports more than 1,500 SCM undergraduates; Haslam reports 9,816 total undergraduates in Fall 2025. SCM is therefore at least 15.3% (`1,500 / 9,816`), or at least one SCM student for every 5.54 other Haslam undergraduates. Haslam also calls SCM UT's No. 1 major by degrees granted from 2020–2024. Confidence: high; the numerator is rounded down by “more than.”

### Curriculum and flexibility
Required: SCM 309 Supply Chain Analytics and Decision Making; SCM 413 Logistics Operations; SCM 414 Manufacturing and Service Operations; SCM 421 Global Strategic and Sustainable Sourcing; SCM 460 Global Strategies for SCM; SCM 489 Internship in SCM. Published pathways include Business Analytics and Information Management concentrations, a collateral option, Heath Integrated Business and Engineering, and international-business combinations. The internship course is zero credit. Haslam emphasizes pathway breadth rather than an MSU-style catalog of one- and two-credit SCM topic workshops.

### Experience, services, paths, voices
An internship or equivalent is required: at least 150 relevant hours in less than six months plus SCM 489 documentation and a reflection video. Internships, co-ops, relevant jobs, micro-internships, and international internships can qualify. Support includes SCM career coaching, Walk-in Wednesday, Handshake, fall/spring recruiting, speed networking, the Supply Chain Forum, Career Ready Certification, and Scholars of Distinction. More than 150 companies recruit annually. Advertised roles include area manager, logistics/procurement specialist, transportation analyst, project manager, consultant, and rotational leadership programs. Lindsey Cauble's official spotlight shows exploration: she combined SCM with accounting, pursued an internship to test the field, and hoped to connect both majors with sports operations. Haslam internship research says students valued meaningful work, skill development, trust, and lasting connections.

### Outcomes
The current outcomes page reports 94% employed or continuing education, 96% internship participation, and $63,085 average salary. Haslam overall reports 93%, 87%, and $61,276. SCM salary is sixth among 10 displayed majors and $1,809 (3.0%) above the college average. A separate program page still shows 95% and $61,909; the dashboard uses the cross-major table and flags the conflict.

## Arizona State — W. P. Carey School of Business

### SCM and OM relationship
W. P. Carey offers a STEM-designated B.S. in Supply Chain Management at Tempe, Online, and ASU Local, plus a B.A. Business (Applied Supply Chains) at West Valley, Online, and ASU Local. There is no separate undergraduate OM major; OM is embedded in the B.S.

### Enrollment estimate
W. P. Carey reports 23,537 undergraduates but not SCM headcount. A bounded proxy uses recent IPEDS-style completions: ASU Digital reports 47 SCM bachelor's completions while 107 were reported statewide across four institutions. Placing combined ASU campus/online completions plausibly at 75–107 and multiplying by four yields roughly 300–430 majors, 1.3–1.8% of W. P. Carey, or one SCM major per 55–77 other undergraduates. Confidence: low; pathway and modality reporting make the proxy imperfect.

### Curriculum and flexibility
The 2025–26 B.S. checksheet requires SCM 315 Supply Chain Analytics, SCM 345 Logistics Management, SCM 355 Procurement and Supply Management, SCM 432 Operations Management, SCM 455 Research and Negotiation, and SCM 479 Supply Chain Strategy (18 credits), plus SCM 300 in the business core and WPC 480 capstone. A notable add-on is four required quarter-credit career courses—WPC 148, 248/347, 348, and 448—one credit across four years. Students can add minors, certificates, concurrent degrees, or the applied B.A.

### Experience, services, paths, voices
No universal internship requirement is published. Available experiences include case projects, elective internship credit, a six-month undergraduate co-op, capstone work, Business Community Advantage, student organizations, the Executive Consortium, and scholarships. Career Services provides assigned coaches, a staged roadmap, employer events, and recruiting support. Advertised roles include logistics specialist, purchasing administrator, supply chain/procurement analyst, manager, and consultant. Public undergraduate SCM testimonials are sparse; official undergraduate stories more generally credit career coaches and older students with helping locate internships. The dashboard states this evidence gap rather than using graduate testimonials.

### Outcomes
W. P. Carey publishes a Class of 2025 schoolwide undergraduate starting salary of $59,112, but no current undergraduate SCM salary or employment table. An SCM careers page says placement is “close to 100 percent” without cohort, denominator, or window; it is treated as advertising, not a comparable outcome. Major salary rank is unavailable.

## Penn State — Smeal College of Business

### SCM and OM relationship
Smeal offers a B.S. in Supply Chain and Information Systems (SCIS), not separate SCM and OM majors. Operations is integrated with sourcing, demand fulfillment, analytics, and IT-enabled coordination. The information-systems component is more explicit than at the other peers.

### Enrollment estimate
The 2023–24 outcomes report shows 325 SCIS graduates with known outcomes at an 86% knowledge rate, implying about 378 graduates (`325 / .86`). A four-year pipeline yields roughly 1,510 SCIS majors. The report covers 1,981 graduating business students, so SCIS represents about 19.1% of the graduating-major mix, or one SCIS student per 4.23 other Smeal students. Confidence: medium; it is a flow-to-stock estimate assuming reasonably stable cohorts.

### Curriculum and flexibility
Required: SCM 301 Supply Chain Management, SCM 404 Demand Fulfillment, SCM 405 Manufacturing and Services Strategies, SCM 406 Strategic Procurement, SCM 421 Supply Chain Analytics, and SCM 450W Strategic Design and Management of Supply Chains. MIS 250 supports the IS side. Students retain three related credits, six supporting credits, and 14 open electives. Smeal offers professional-development workshops, but not a published MSU-like recurring menu of low-credit SCM topic workshops.

### Experience, services, paths, voices
An internship is not a graduation requirement. Real-world exposure includes cases, visiting leaders, company research, study abroad, and industry partnerships. The Center for Supply Chain Research connects students with more than 35 companies and runs fall/spring SCIS career fairs; the bulletin reports more than 6,000 student/recruiter interactions across five fair days. Career services add coaching, mock fairs, networking expos, résumé/interview resources, job boards, and employer relations. Advertised roles include materials scheduler/analyst, production/logistics analyst, procurement, demand planning, and consulting. Kunjal Kapadia secured an Estée Lauder procurement internship through the SCIS fair. Connor MacEachern said he chose SCIS partly because he knew little about supply chain and wanted to learn; he highlighted Smeal's support system.

### Outcomes
The 2023–24 report gives SCIS 86% placement, $73,022 average salary, $6,070 signing bonus, 57% receiving a signing bonus, and 88% with at least one internship. Salary ranks fifth of 11 displayed majors and essentially matches the $73,027 business mean. Known outcomes: 325; knowledge rate: 86%. The linked 2024–25 report requires SharePoint access, so 2023–24 is the latest publicly inspectable table.

## Georgia Tech — Scheller College of Business

### SCM and OM relationship
Scheller awards one B.S. in Business Administration. Operations and Supply Chain Management (OSCM) is an 18-credit concentration, not a standalone major. Every BSBA student completes one concentration, and only one concentration is officially recognized.

### Enrollment estimate
Scheller reports 1,509 undergraduates but no concentration headcount. With eight reported concentrations, an equal-share baseline is 189. A deliberately broad 10–15% scenario gives roughly 150–225 OSCM students, or one OSCM student per 5.7–9 other undergraduates. Confidence: low; this is a scenario range, not observed distribution.

### Curriculum and flexibility
After core MGT 3501 Operations Management, students complete six advanced OSCM courses (18 credits). Four come from Management of Technology; Product, Service and Technology Development; Operations Resource Planning and Execution; Operations Strategy; Global Operations and SCM; Service Operations; and Supply Chain Modeling. Two come from a broader approved group. The BSBA also requires MGT 3599 Career Development Workshop (one credit). Certificates can add depth, with limits on duplicate areas and double counting.

### Experience, services, paths, voices
Internships are available but not universally required; up to three credits may count. The four-year plan can accommodate two internship semesters and a term abroad. Options include co-ops, undergraduate research, independent study, global programs, the Supply Chain and Logistics Organization, employer events, and Tech Square. Career support centers on required MGT 3599, advisors, résumé/interview coaching, fairs, and alumni/employer programming. Scheller frames the concentration for operations, logistics, analytics/modeling, technology management, service operations, and consulting. Inika Jain highlighted the intersection of business and technology and the usefulness of tech-enabled operations; this is an individual promotional story.

### Outcomes
For the Class of 2025, Scheller reports 93% securing full-time employment within three months and an $82,722 overall mean. OSCM's mean is $71,266, sixth of eight concentrations and $11,456 (13.9%) below the overall mean. Concentration employment and sample size are not published. A separate page says 90% and gives a different signing bonus; the dated Class of 2025 report is used.

## Cross-school cautions

- “Placement,” “employed,” and “positive destination” are different constructs.
- Tennessee and ASU marketing figures lack the knowledge-rate detail of MSU and Penn State.
- ASU does not publish a current undergraduate SCM outcome table.
- Georgia Tech publishes concentration salary but not concentration placement or sample size.
- Enrollment confidence is high for MSU and Tennessee, medium for Penn State, and low for ASU and Georgia Tech.
- Testimonials explain themes and mechanisms; they do not establish prevalence or causality.

### University of Pennsylvania — Wharton

- **Structure:** all undergraduates earn a BS in Economics. - **Careers/outcomes:** consulting, manufacturing, technology, analytics and operations are advertised. ## Comparison-page methodology

The dashboard’s comparison view classifies all 16 schools into three mutually exclusive groups:

- **SCM major only (6):** Michigan State, Tennessee, Arizona State, Penn State, UT Austin, and UT Dallas. Each has a named undergraduate SCM degree (including Penn State’s SCM/Information Systems combination) and no separate OM degree.
- **Both SCM and OM (4):** Purdue and Minnesota use explicitly combined SCM–OM majors; Indiana and Illinois offer separate SCM and OM majors.
- **OM or related only, without an SCM major (6):** Georgia Tech, Michigan Ross, Ohio State, Wharton, Carnegie Mellon, and MIT. This group includes OSCM/OM concentrations, OID, logistics specializations, and Ross’s uncredentialed elective/career pathway.

The comparison table reproduces the enrollment figures already documented in the individual profiles and adds filters for architecture, evidence level, and school name. “Published count” is reserved for a direct institutional count or floor; “derived” uses a published cohort or degree share; “estimate/scenario” relies on a bounded proxy; and “proxy/not comparable” identifies a measure that is not enrollment. Business-school totals, field counts, shares, and ratios retain their original denominators and periods, so the table should be used for scale and structure rather than a mechanically ranked league table.
